================================================================================
1. THE QUESTION
================================================================================

SEC Rule 14a-8(i)(12) lets a company omit a shareholder proposal that has
failed before. The bar depends on how many times the proposal has been voted on
within the preceding five calendar years:

    voted once            -> must have received at least  5%
    voted twice           -> must have received at least 15%
    voted three or more   -> must have received at least 25%

Below the bar, the company may exclude the next submission. Above it, it may not
exclude on this ground.

The study asks whether this rule deters proponents from returning. The design is
a regression discontinuity: proposals just below the bar are excludable, those
just above are not, and nothing else should differ sharply at the line.

The thresholds were 3%/6%/10% before 1 January 2022 and 5%/15%/25% after. This
single fact caused the largest correction in the whole project (Section 5.4).

5.4 THE SECOND AND WORST BUG — wrong thresholds for 2021

Of the 334 observations in the 15% sample, 81 (24%) were 2021 votes. But the
5%/15%/25% schedule only governs meetings on or after 1 January 2022. Before
that the bars were 3%/6%/10%.

add_threshold_variables never looked at the filing date. It mapped
RESUBMISSION_THRESHOLDS purely by vote_number_5y:

    n = df["vote_number_5y"].clip(lower=1, upper=3).astype(int)
    df["applicable_threshold"] = n.map(RESUBMISSION_THRESHOLDS)
    df["run_var"] = df["vote_share"] - df["applicable_threshold"]
    df["below_threshold"] = df["vote_share"] < df["applicable_threshold"]

So a 2021 vote at 12% was scored against a 15% bar, when the law in force said
10%. Those rows were assigned to the wrong side of the line entirely.

Fix: analysis window moved to 2022-01-01 .. 2025-12-31. The date filter sits
after parsing, so the rerun cost only 95 HTTP requests against 3,528 cache hits
and finished in 597 seconds.

5.5 Corrected run — window 2022-01-01 to 2025-12-31 (authoritative)

  3,040 discovered, 3,040 fetched, 2,905 parsed, 135 skipped, 0 fetch errors
  37,653 proposal rows, 4,852 shareholder-proposal rows, 2,294 valid
  15% sample: 323 (94 below, 229 at or above)
  covariate coverage 100%
  years: 2022 67, 2023 89, 2024 97, 2025 70
  earliest filing 2022-03-14

5.6 A structural detail that caused a false alarm later

add_resubmission_variables runs on the FULL parsed set, before the date filter.
So priors from 2017-2021 count toward vote_number_5y but are absent from the
exported rdd_data.csv.

Consequence: the CSV alone cannot reproduce vote_number_5y. An early attempt to
recompute the linkage from the CSV alone showed 45.7% of rows changing bar. That
figure was an artifact of the missing pre-2022 priors and was discarded. The
real figure, computed on the full universe, is about 4% (Section 10.3).

Assumption A4: vote_share is computed from raw counts, never printed
percentages. VERIFIED. votes_for and votes_against have zero nulls across all
2,294 rows. No row has vote_share exactly equal to 0.05, 0.15 or 0.25; only
three values sit at two decimal places. The rounding-at-the-cutoff worry is
therefore dead, and this is the single most important thing to have gotten
right.

Assumption A5: abstentions and broker non-votes are excluded from the
denominator, per SEC Staff Legal Bulletin No. 14. The denominator is For plus
Against.

Assumption A6: the comparison is strict. 14.99% is excludable, 15.00% is not.
Implemented as a strict `<`, and covered by a self-test.

Assumption A7: for a proposal on its second appearance, comparing that vote's
share to 15% is exactly the bar governing exclusion of the third submission.
Confirmed correct. This is a subtle point and the code gets it right.

Assumption A8: history_complete is True for all usable rows. VERIFIED for all
2,052.

02_rdd_analysis.py was written and debugged. Four bugs, all in the Python port's
API rather than the statistics:

  BUG 2  rddensity r.test is a pandas Series indexed
         ['t_asy','t_jk','p_asy','p_jk'], not a dict with a 'test' key. The
         asymptotic entries are NaN under the default binned setup; the
         jackknife pair is the one to report.
  BUG 3  r.n and r.h are Series with named indices ('full','left','right',...).
         Integer indexing raised KeyError: 0.
  BUG 4  bwselect="manual" raises "bwselect incorrectly specified". The valid
         list is mserd/msetwo/msesum/msecomb1/msecomb2/cerrd/... and an EMPTY
         STRING signals manual.
  BUG 5  pandas iterrows upcast integers to floats, breaking "{:d}" formatting.

Single-cutoff result at 15%, n=292:
  McCrary t=0.830 p=0.407  PASS
  h*=0.0677, bias-corrected tau=0.137, SE 0.313, p 0.663
  bandwidth grid: sign stable across 0.5x-2x
  covariate balance: all pass
  placebo cutoffs at 10% and 20%: null
  polynomial p=1 vs p=2: stable

The problem: MDE at 80% power was 87.8 percentage points. Only 24 observations
sat within +/-2pp of the cutoff, 71 inside the bandwidth. Detecting a realistic
10pp effect would have required roughly 77 times more effective observations.

This is not a bug. It is a property of the design at a single threshold, and it
became the paper's central motivation rather than a buried failure.

Right-censoring was found here: 31 of the 323 rows have a null refiled_t1, all
from filing year 2025, because their next annual meeting has not happened. The
main specification drops them (n=292); a robustness block reinstates them as
zeros and the sign is unchanged.

7. PHASE 5 — POOLING THE THREE BARS (the headline)
================================================================================

7.1 The idea

All three bars have the same structure: a running variable measured as vote
share minus the applicable bar. Stacking them takes the sample from 298 to
2,052 and observations within +/-2pp from 29 to 153.

7.2 Two structural discoveries

  DISCOVERY 1  vote_number_5y runs from 1 to 65, not 1 to 3. Recurring
  proposals (lobbying disclosure and similar) accumulate appearances, and the
  law caps the bar at 25% for three or more. So the groups are n==1 -> 5%,
  n==2 -> 15%, n>=3 -> 25%.

  DISCOVERY 2  Only 431 unique firms, with up to 85 rows from a single firm.
  Unclustered standard errors were badly overstated. All specifications now
  cluster on CIK.

7.3 Main estimates (bias-corrected, clustered on CIK)

SUPERSEDED — these are the pre-fix tokenizer numbers, kept only to show what was
originally reported. The current estimates are in Section 12.2 and are roughly
half this size.

  pooled, threshold FE                  0.158  SE 0.081  p 0.052
  pooled, threshold FE + covariates     0.182  SE 0.084  p 0.030
  pooled, no adjustment                 0.148  SE 0.088  p 0.092

Per threshold (covariates + cluster):
  5% bar   tau 0.030  SE 0.092  p 0.742
  15% bar  tau 0.166  SE 0.286  p 0.562
  25% bar  tau 0.208  SE 0.218  p 0.339

Current per threshold (post-fix, see Section 12.2):
  5% bar   tau -0.030  SE 0.088  p 0.735
  15% bar  tau  0.110  SE 0.246  p 0.654
  25% bar  tau -0.004  SE 0.224  p 0.985

7.4 Assumption A9 — the one the pooled design lives on

Pooling assumes the effect of becoming eligible for exclusion is comparable
across the three bars. Threshold fixed effects absorb differences in the LEVEL
of refiling between groups; they cannot fix differences in the SLOPE of the
effect. The per-bar point estimates (0.03, 0.17, 0.21) suggest the effect grows
with the bar, which pooling understates. This is stated in the paper rather
than hidden.

7.5 The pooled density test is a known artifact

  per bar:  5% t=0.634 p=0.526 | 15% t=0.830 p=0.407 | 25% t=0.245 p=0.806
  pooled:   t=1.921 p=0.055

The pooled figure is meaningless. Stacking three groups with different vote
share distributions creates a mechanical density jump at zero that has nothing
to do with manipulation. Each bar passes individually. The paper says so
explicitly and never reports the pooled number as evidence of sorting.

7.6 Assumption A10 — threshold assignment depends on topic matching

vote_number_5y is built by matching proposal titles across years with Jaccard
similarity >= 0.5. This is a proxy for "substantially the same subject matter".
Errors here assign the wrong CUTOFF, not merely a noisy outcome. Section 10
covers what was found when this was audited.

================================================================================
10. PHASE 8 — REFEREE AUDIT AND THE CORRECTIONS IT FORCED
================================================================================

The whole foundation was then audited as a hostile referee would. Six
substantive problems were found. All six are being fixed; this section records
what they were.

10.1 The outcome was misdescribed (most serious)

refiled_t1 is 1 where a similar proposal was VOTED ON 6-18 months later. An
excluded resubmission produces no vote record, so it is invisible and scores 0.

Therefore the outcome is:

    P(same topic reaches a ballot next year) = P(refiled AND included)

The paper had it exactly backwards, stating that the outcome records whether a
proposal was filed rather than whether it reached a ballot. It also described
the company's inclusion decision as "a separate, unmeasured margin", which is
false: it is inside the outcome.

Fixed. The estimand is now stated correctly as the reduced-form effect on
BALLOT ACCESS. This is arguably the better and more policy-relevant object,
because the rule exists to keep proposals off ballots. But it is not an estimate
of proponent behaviour alone, and the paper now says so.

10.2 No first stage, so the mechanism is unidentified

Exclusion is never observed. The coefficient cannot be decomposed into companies
excluding (supply) and proponents anticipating exclusion and not filing (demand).
These have different policy implications. Resolving this requires SEC no-action
letters. This remains the single largest open item (Section 12).

10.3 Threshold assignment is fragile under topic-matching choices

The tokenizer stripped proposal vocabulary but not vote-result boilerplate.
50.4% of descriptions contain such boilerplate, rising to 83.4% of the 25% bar.
Example:

    "The stockholder proposal regarding political spending disclosure as
     described in the Company's 2022 proxy statement was not approved as
     follows:"

Rebuilding the linkage with that boilerplate stripped moves about 4.3% of bar
assignments in the full universe (2022-2025, all proposals) and about 10.3% in
the final shareholder-proposal sample. Because boilerplate prevalence differs
sharply by bar, the error is correlated with treatment assignment.

Fixed: _TOPIC_STOP rewritten into two documented families, structural
vocabulary and vote-result boilerplate, with content words such as report and
policy deliberately retained. The whole pipeline was re-run from the warm cache
so that vote_number_5y and refiled_t1 are recomputed, not patched.

10.4 Sample completeness was asserted, never validated

Discovery requires an 8-K with Item 5.07 AND one of three phrases in the full
text. No external benchmark had been checked.

Validated for 2023: 362 filings use "shareholder proposal", 613 use
"stockholder proposal"; 49.7% and 32.7% respectively carry Item 5.07, implying
roughly 340 eligible filings. We discovered 319, a capture rate near 94%. The
items metadata was confirmed reliable (empty for 0.0% of sampled hits).

10.5 Missing controls

Year is imbalanced across bars (25% bar: 160 rows in 2022 vs 100 in 2025), and
topic composition differs by bar (the 25% bar is dominated by persistent
lobbying proposals). Neither year fixed effects nor topic fixed effects were in
any specification. The 25% bar also pooled n==3 with n==65.

Fixed: 02c_robustness.py adds year FE, topic FE, both together, a
covariates-free variant, a 25%-bar-restricted-to-n==3 variant, and a variant
dropping the 5% bar entirely. Duplicates on (cik, filing_date, proposal_desc)
are dropped.

10.6 The headline was the most favourable of three specifications

FE only p=0.052, FE+covariates p=0.030, no adjustment p=0.092. Only one clears
0.05 and it has the most free parameters. The paper now reports the range rather
than a single number, and the robustness suite is presented as a distribution of
estimates rather than a hunt for stars.

Also flagged but not yet fixed: MSERD bandwidth selection is derived for
independent sampling, so pairing it with clustering on CIK is not covered by the
theory. Mitigation is to report the fixed-bandwidth grid alongside h* and not to
lean on h* as if it were optimal under clustering.

Also noted: 20 exact duplicate rows existed in the usable sample, and some
descriptions are truncated to no topic at all, e.g.
"Greenhill's stockholders voted against the Stockholder Proposal:" - these
cannot be matched meaningfully.

12. PHASE 10 — THE MATCHING THRESHOLD, AND THE COLLAPSE OF THE HEADLINE
================================================================================

This is the most important correction in the project, and it was found by
testing the audit's own fix rather than trusting it.

12.1 Why it was tested

Fixing the tokenizer (Section 10.3) changes vote_number_5y, and therefore changes
which bar every proposal is measured against. That is not a cosmetic edit. If the
new stoplist were too aggressive, it would break genuine matches and demote real
15%-bar proposals to the 5% bar, producing a spuriously null result. If it were
too weak, the old false matches would survive.

So the matching threshold was made a parameter rather than a constant:

    TOPIC_SIMILARITY_MIN = float(os.environ.get("TOPIC_SIM_MIN", "0.5"))

and the entire pipeline was re-run at 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 from the warm cache.

12.2 What happened

The headline estimate had been 0.182 with p = 0.030 on the pre-fix tokenizer.
After the fix at the same 0.5 threshold it fell to 0.107 with p = 0.151.

Pooled, bias-corrected, clustered on CIK:

    Jaccard   pooled FE           pooled FE + covariates    p
    0.4       0.126 (SE 0.072)    0.140 (SE 0.072)          0.052
    0.5       0.085 (SE 0.073)    0.107 (SE 0.075)          0.151
    0.6       0.138 (SE 0.059)    0.158 (SE 0.058)          0.007

Per bar, the swing is much larger:

    15% bar   0.399 (p 0.041) -> 0.110 (p 0.654) -> 0.450 (p 0.002)
    25% bar   0.058 (p 0.808) -> -0.004 (p 0.985) -> -0.144 (p 0.507)

The 25% bar changes sign. The 15% bar nearly quadruples. Only the pooled
estimate keeps a stable sign.

12.3 What was concluded, and what was thrown away

The old 0.182 result is dead. It was produced by a tokenizer that let vote-result
boilerplate into the topic tokens, inflating prior matches and therefore pushing
proposals into higher bars.

The honest claim is now: the effect is positive in every specification and every
matching threshold, with a pooled range of roughly 0.07 to 0.25, but its
magnitude and significance are not robust. The binding constraint on this study
is the measurement of the threshold assignment, not the sample size.

Consequences applied to the paper:

  - Abstract rewritten around 0.107 (SE 0.075, p 0.151), framed as suggestive.
  - Section 4.4 updated to the new single-cutoff numbers (298 proposals, 29
    within 2pp, 0.116 with SE 0.270, p 0.668, MDE ~76pp).
  - Section 5.1 rewritten, explicitly stating the estimate is about half the
    preliminary version and that the earlier figure must not be cited.
  - Section 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 5.5, 5.6, 5.7 all re-tabulated.
  - New Section 5.8 added, with 5.8a (seven robustness specifications) and
    5.8b (three matching thresholds).
  - The claim of a monotone gradient across bars was RETRACTED. The per-bar
    estimates are now -0.030, 0.110 and -0.004, which is no pattern at all.
  - Section 6 measurement paragraph rewritten to distinguish outcome noise from
    cutoff misassignment, and to name the latter as the binding limitation.
  - Section 7 conclusion rewritten as suggestive evidence.
  - Section 3 sample table updated to 2,052 observations, 430 firms.
  - README and NEXT_STEPS rewritten, with an explicit superseded notice.

12.4 The lesson worth keeping

A robustness check that only confirms what you already found is not a check.
The single most useful thing done in this project was varying the one parameter
that the whole identification depends on, and reporting the result when it moved.

Assumption A10 (topic matching identifies "substantially the same subject
matter") is therefore not merely imperfect: it is the dominant source of
uncertainty in the estimates, and it is now stated that way.

13. OPEN ITEMS
================================================================================

  1. HAND-VALIDATE THE LINKAGE (now the highest-value item). Draw a random
     sample of matched and unmatched pairs and hand-code whether they are the
     same subject matter. That would establish whether 0.4, 0.5 or 0.6 is
     closest to truth, and would convert the current range of estimates into a
     single defensible number with a measurement-error correction. Everything
     else on this list is secondary to it.

  2. FIRST STAGE. Scrape SEC no-action letters to observe whether exclusion
     actually happened. Without it the coefficient cannot be split into company
     exclusion versus proponent deterrence. New data collection.

  3. Verify or delete the two claims about no-action relief drying up and the
     proposed rescission of Rule 14a-8. Marked with a TODO in the paper.

  4. Proponent identity is not parsed, so sponsor fixed effects are impossible
     as built. Would require parsing the proponent name from each filing.

  5. MSERD bandwidth selection under clustering is not theory-supported.
     Reported as a robustness grid instead.

  6. Descriptions truncated to no topic should be counted and dropped
     explicitly rather than matched arbitrarily.

  7. The 94% coverage figure is for 2023 only. Other years are assumed similar
     and not measured.

WHY THIS IS WITHDRAWN RATHER THAN CORRECTED.
The series trends down across the whole sample. A 188-day change is therefore
not a stationary quantity: its distribution depends entirely on where the
window sits relative to the trend, and the observed window sits at the end.
Restricting draws to windows that end before the election does not fix this,
it makes the comparison worse -- it compares the observed window, which
contains the trend's endpoint, against windows that by construction cannot.
The p-value that comes out is a statement about the shape of the series, not
about the election. It is reported here, and in the paper, as a failed test.
The placebo evidence the paper relies on is the placebo-date test on a panel
truncated before the event, which is a real test and passes.

11. BOND-IMPLIED VERSUS MARKET-IMPLIED PROBABILITY
============================================================================
If the spread moves one-for-one with the probability that Orban leaves office,
the bond market's implied probability is (S0 - St) / (S0 - ST).

That maps the 4 August spread to zero, but the contract was
already at 0.625 that day. The two series do not start in the same place:
one is a probability level, the other a share of the eventual fall.
level, the other a share of the eventual fall, and setting them against each
other hands the bond market a free head start by construction. The like-for-like
comparison rescales both by their own eventual change, so that both are zero on
4 August and one on 8 May:

    share_t = (x_t - x_0) / (x_T - x_0),   x = contract price or spread

date           spread       P   contract    bonds     gap
2025-08-04       2.76   0.625      0.000    0.000  +0.000
2026-02-26       2.35   0.625      0.000    0.259  -0.259
2026-03-31       2.53   0.655      0.080    0.148  -0.068
2026-04-09       2.18   0.685      0.160    0.366  -0.206
2026-04-12       2.06   0.685      0.160    0.441  -0.281
2026-05-08       1.16   1.000      1.000    1.000  +0.000

On 9 April the contract had delivered 16% of its own move and the spread
37%, a gap of 21 points in the spread's favour.
On the eve of the vote it is 16% against 44%: the gap widens to
28 points, because the spread kept falling after the
contract had stopped repricing. In March the two move against each other: the
spread retraced to its early-August level in the global selloff (-4% on
24 March) while the contract held at 5%. That is a give-back on both
sides, not a lead in either direction.

So the contract did not anticipate the bonds in this window. Over the eight
months to the vote it travelled from 0.625 to 0.685 while the spread did a
third of its eventual move, and it did not move decisively until the count.
The ordering is not robust to the choice of base date, however: anchored on
the contract's opening week, which is when it repriced 26 points on seven
prints, the same calculation puts the contract at 49 per cent and the spread
at 35. The comparison therefore measures how quickly each market moved once
it started, not which one started first, and it is not a lead-lag test.

Read the bonds columns as shares, not probabilities. Under the 13 November
anchor the share exceeds one after May 2026 because the spread keeps narrowing
past the terminal value assumed for it: 1.438 on 13 November means the spread
overshot the 8 May level by 44 per cent of the total fall. A share can do that;
a probability cannot. That anchor is kept in the table as a robustness check and
is not plotted, because it leaves the 0-1 axis.
